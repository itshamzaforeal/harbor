The approach

Steadiness first. Everything else follows.

Three ideas shape how coaching works at Harbor — not rules, just the things we keep coming back to.

On steadiness

Steady isn't the same as fixed.

You don't need to overhaul your life, your job, or your habits to feel like yourself again. Most people who come to Harbor don't need a new life — they need firmer ground under the one they have.

On rhythm

Rhythm beats breakthroughs.

A single big insight rarely holds on its own. What holds is showing up at the same time each week, in small enough steps that they actually stick — the ground gets built one ordinary session at a time.

On perspective

Calm is contagious.

When someone steady sits across from you, week after week, it changes what feels possible — not because they solve it for you, but because their calm becomes a place you can borrow from until you have your own.

How it works

Four stages, roughly in this order — though the pace is set by what you're carrying, not a fixed calendar.

A free call

Getting your bearings

The first two or three sessions map what's actually pulling you off balance — patterns, pressures, the gap between how things look and how they feel day to day.

SESSIONS 1–3
03

Weekly rhythm

Regular sessions, same time each week. Small, specific adjustments between them — not homework for its own sake, but small tests of steadier ways to move through your week.

ONGOING, WEEKLY
04

Ongoing steadiness

As the ground holds, sessions taper — every other week, then monthly check-ins, or you keep the weekly rhythm as long as it's useful. There's no finish line to rush toward.
